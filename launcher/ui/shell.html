    How much the Pērkons can do at once

    Processor and memory

    Work in progress. This is what we know so far, not the final word. The numbers and advice come from measurements and listening on a Pērkons, and they will change as we learn more and as the engines are made lighter. Treat them as a guide, and trust your ears.

    Every mod and engine you add takes some of the Pērkons's memory, and an engine takes processor time while it plays. The Mod Matrix looks after memory for you. The processor is yours to manage: how much it takes depends on which engines play on which voices and how they're set, and that changes from moment to moment.

    Memory MANAGED FOR YOU

    The MEMORY pane in the Mod Matrix shows how much room the mods you tick take. A mod that wouldn't fit can't be ticked, and the build checks again. A build that fits always fits: memory doesn't change with what you play.

    Processor UP TO YOU

    No one can check it for you before you play: it depends on what's playing. Too much shows up as crackle on voices 1 and 2. Put the heavy engines on voices 3 and 4, and use the planner below to check a kit.

    Memory

    The Pērkons has three blocks of memory that mods can use. The MEMORY pane in the Mod Matrix has a meter for each. Grey is the firmware's own part, green is what your ticked mods take, and FREE is what's left.

    • A mod you can't tick doesn't fit beside the ones already ticked. Untick another mod to make room.
    • An engine in your build costs memory, not processor time. It only uses the processor while it's chosen on a voice. Install the engines you like; choose them per kit.

    Processor

    The Pērkons has two processors. The panel processor runs the panel, pages, sequencer, kit saving, the SD card and the effects (the delay, FX Reverb, FX Freeze). The sound processor works out the sound of the four voices, and only that work competes with the voices.

    It works in tiny blocks: every 32 samples, about two thirds of a millisecond, it works out the next bit of sound for all four voices. It does voices 3 and 4 first (with the step triggers), then voices 1 and 2, and everything has to be ready before the block is due. If it isn't, voices 1 and 2, done last, come out broken, while voices 3 and 4 stay clean.

    Why the Mod Matrix can't check it. Memory is fixed when you build, so the Mod Matrix can add it up. Processor time depends on what's playing: which engine is on each voice (you choose that on the Pērkons, per kit), the preset, how many notes, drive, filter settings. The same build can play one kit cleanly and crackle on another.

    Signs of too much processor

    • Crackle, grit, clicks or dropouts on voice 1 or 2 only, while voices 3 and 4 stay clean. Mute voices one at a time to find which one breaks.
    • It comes and goes with how busy the heavy engines are: bigger chords, more oscillators, drive on, held notes overlapping, several heavy voices sounding at once.
    • It starts with a kit or engine change rather than when you turn something up.
    • It stops when you mute a heavy voice, choose a lighter preset, or move a heavy engine to voice 3 or 4.
    Too loud, not too busy. Crackle on loud, held sounds that goes away when you turn down the voice's LEVEL, the MASTER, or the compressor's Amount and Threshold is the analog output running out of headroom, not the processor. Turn those down instead (the Akords guide has more on this, "Crackle on held chords").

    What to do

    1. Move the heavy engine to voice 3 or 4. Hold KIT and press MOD for the engine page: rows 1 to 4 are the voices. The engine then plays from that voice's steps, trigger key and output, so move its steps too.
    2. Choose a lighter sound: fewer notes in a chord, a lighter preset, drive off.
    3. Swap one heavy engine for a light one or an official engine.

    Where to put engines

    Voices 1 and 2 done last

    The official engines and light engines (Zibens, Step Sampler playing forward or reverse). At most one medium or heavy engine here, beside a light one.

    Voices 3 and 4 done first

    The medium, heavy and very heavy engines: Akords, Synth Lab, Kick Lab with its effects on, Step Sampler on granular.

    • Avoid two heavy engines on voices 1 and 2 together, Akords especially.
    • Four heavy engines at once is too much wherever they are. The planner below shows how much a kit takes.
    • An engine costs nothing while it isn't chosen on a voice, so a build can include all of them.

      How these numbers were found

      Each engine was run with the real firmware in an emulator, counting the work it does per sample, and the limit was found by ear on a Pērkons, with real engines playing. The four official voices together use about half of what played clean. The numbers are a guide: the chip also loses some time to memory, and the limit between "played clean" and "broke" hasn't been tested, so leave some room.
